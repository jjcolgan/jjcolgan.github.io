About

John (JJ) Colgan

I am a senior graduate student in the Chang and Blekhman labs at the University of Chicago, where my research sits at the intersection of microbiology, genomics, and host-microbe biology. With over eight years of research experience spanning ecology, bioinformatics, and micro-, molecular and systems biology, I bring a broad and integrative perspective to the study of the gut microbiome.

My work focuses on identifying the bacterial genes that govern microbial engraftment across distinct regions of the gastrointestinal tract — particularly the mechanistic differences between colonization of the small and large intestine. By integrating large multi-omic datasets, I aim to uncover the molecular interactions through which host and microbe establish mutually beneficial systems, and to understand how regional host-microbe homeostasis is maintained across the intestinal tract.

In addition to my work in graduate school, I have served as a lead computer science instructor for 4 years with Project SYNCERE, a non-profit which provides supplemental STEM instruction to underserved youths in the Chicagoland area.

In my free time, I like to explore Chicago, lounge by Lake Michigan, play guitar, learn new recipies and relax with my Chihuahuas, Suzy and Randall.

Research Interests

  • Niche directed enteric bacterial engraftment
  • Regional host-microbiome interactions along the intestinal tract
  • Multi-omic data integration (genomics, transcriptomics, metagenomics, metabolomics)
  • Host-microbe co-evolution and mutualistic systems
  • Bioinformatics, computational biology and machine learning
